Can Depression Counselling Help When Nothing Feels Enjoyable?

A gentle, practical guide from the BetterLyf team · 7 min read

You used to love your morning chai, your weekend plans, maybe even your work. Now everything feels flat, like someone turned the colour down on life.

If that sounds familiar, you may be asking: can depression counselling help when nothing feels enjoyable? The short answer is yes. Losing interest in things you once loved is one of the most common signs of depression, and it is also one of the most treatable. At BetterLyf, we see this every day. In this guide, we explain why joy fades, how counselling works, and how to take a first small step today.

Why Does Nothing Feel Enjoyable When You Are Depressed?

Doctors call this loss of pleasure anhedonia. It is not laziness, and it is not a character flaw. Depression affects the brain systems that handle motivation, energy and reward, so activities that once felt good simply stop giving you that lift.

Many people describe it in similar ways:

  • Hobbies, food, music or friends no longer feel rewarding
  • Small tasks feel heavy, even getting out of bed
  • You feel numb instead of sad
  • You say yes to plans, then cancel at the last minute
  • Guilt grows because you feel you should be enjoying life

Remember: You are not ungrateful or broken. Your mind is exhausted, and it needs support, not blame.

How Depression Counselling Helps When Joy Feels Gone

Depression counselling gives you a steady, judgement-free space to talk with a trained professional. You do not need to feel motivated to begin. The counsellor carries much of the effort at first, which is a real relief when your own energy is low.

1. It helps you understand what is happening

Simply learning that numbness is a symptom, not a personal failure, can lift a surprising amount of shame. Your counsellor explains how mood, thoughts and daily habits feed each other.

2. It challenges the harsh voice in your head

Depression often whispers things like “nothing will ever change.” Approaches such as cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) teach you to notice these thoughts and test them gently, so they lose their grip.

3. It rebuilds enjoyment in tiny steps

A technique called behavioural activation starts small: a five-minute walk, a favourite song, one text to a friend. You do not wait to feel better first. Action comes first, and feeling follows slowly.

4. It gives you someone in your corner

Being truly heard is powerful. Many clients tell us that simply having a weekly space where they can be honest makes the rest of the week feel lighter.

Is Online Counselling as Effective as Meeting in Person?

For many people with depression, online counselling works just as well, and sometimes better. When leaving the house feels impossible, a video or audio session from your own room removes a big barrier.

  • Convenience: No travel, no waiting rooms, flexible timings
  • Privacy: Speak freely from a place where you feel safe
  • Consistency: Easier to keep sessions even on low-energy days
  • Wider choice: Connect with a counsellor who truly suits you, wherever you live

At BetterLyf, our online counselling is built around this idea: support should meet you where you are, not ask you to be somewhere you cannot reach yet.

What to Expect in Your First BetterLyf Session

Starting can feel scary, so here is what really happens. There is no pressure to perform or explain everything perfectly.

  1. A warm welcome. Your counsellor introduces themselves and explains how sessions work and how your privacy is protected.
  2. Your story, at your pace. Share what you are feeling, when it began and what has changed. Skip anything you are not ready to discuss.
  3. Naming the pattern. Together you look at sleep, energy, thoughts and daily routine to understand what keeps you stuck.
  4. A small, kind plan. You agree on one or two realistic goals, such as a short walk or a regular sleep time.
  5. Regular check-ins. Over the next sessions, you review progress, adjust the plan and celebrate small wins.

Small Things You Can Try Today

Counselling works best alongside gentle daily habits. None of these are cures, but they can open a little space while you get support:

  • Step outside for sunlight, even for five minutes
  • Keep a regular wake-up and bedtime routine
  • Do one tiny task, then stop, and count it as a win
  • Message one trusted person without needing a long conversation
  • Write down one thing that felt even slightly less flat today

When Should You Reach Out?

Consider booking depression counselling if low mood or loss of interest has lasted more than two weeks, or if it is affecting your sleep, work, appetite or relationships. You do not need to wait until things feel “bad enough.”

If you are in crisis: If you are thinking about harming yourself, please contact your local emergency number or a trusted person near you right now. You deserve immediate support.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can depression counselling really help if I feel nothing at all?

Yes. Feeling numb is a recognised symptom, and counselling is designed for exactly this. Therapists use small, structured steps that do not rely on you feeling motivated first.

How long does it take to start feeling better?

It varies. Some people notice small shifts within a few weeks, while others need a few months. Steady attendance and honest conversation matter more than speed.

Do I need medication as well?

Not always. Mild to moderate depression often improves with counselling alone. If your counsellor feels medication could help, they will suggest speaking with a doctor or psychiatrist.

Is online counselling confidential?

Yes. Professional counsellors follow strict confidentiality standards. At BetterLyf, your sessions are private and handled with care.

What if I do not know what to say?

That is completely fine. Counsellors guide the conversation with gentle questions. Saying “I do not know why I feel this way” is a perfectly good place to start.

Final Thoughts

When nothing feels enjoyable, it is easy to believe nothing ever will again. But depression is a condition, not your identity, and it responds well to the right support. Depression counselling offers understanding, practical tools and a patient companion for the climb back.
