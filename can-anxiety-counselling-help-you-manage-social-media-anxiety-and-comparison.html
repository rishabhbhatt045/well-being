Why Social Media Can Make Anxiety Worse

Social media is built to keep us scrolling. Every like, comment, and notification gives our brain a tiny reward. Over time, that cycle can leave us restless and checking our phones all day.

It also shows us a filtered version of life. Holidays, promotions, perfect skin, happy couples. We forget that people post their best moments, not their everyday struggles.

Common signs of social media anxiety

  • Feeling a knot in your stomach when you see others' posts
  • Worrying about how many likes or views you get
  • Checking your phone again and again, even when nothing is new
  • Fear of missing out (FOMO) when friends share events
  • Trouble sleeping after late-night scrolling
  • Feeling worse about your body, career, or relationships
Good to know: Comparing yourself to others is a very human habit. The problem starts when it becomes constant and begins to affect your mood, sleep, and self-worth.

What Is the Comparison Trap?

Comparison is how we judge where we stand. In real life, we compare with a few people around us. Online, we compare ourselves with thousands of people, many of them strangers with edited photos and carefully chosen words.

This is an unfair game. You are measuring your behind-the-scenes against someone else's trailer. No wonder it hurts.

When this goes on for months, it can lead to self-doubt, low confidence, and a constant worry that you are falling behind. That is where professional support can make a real difference.

How Anxiety Counselling Helps With Social Media Anxiety

Good anxiety counselling gives you a safe, non-judgmental space to talk about what you feel. You do not have to "just stop using your phone" or "be more positive." Instead, you learn to understand your thoughts and respond to them differently.

1. It helps you spot your triggers

Is it a certain app? A certain person? A certain time of day? Your counsellor helps you notice patterns, so you know exactly what sets off the worry.

2. It teaches you to challenge unhelpful thoughts

Thoughts like "Everyone is doing better than me" feel true, but they are not facts. Using proven approaches such as cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), you learn to question these thoughts and replace them with balanced ones.

3. It builds self-worth from the inside

When your confidence depends on likes, it can crash quickly. Counselling helps you build a steady sense of self that does not rise and fall with every post.

4. It gives you calming tools

You may learn breathing exercises, grounding techniques, and mindful habits. These help your body relax when you feel the urge to scroll or compare.

5. It helps you set healthy boundaries

Your counsellor can guide you in creating a social media routine that suits you, without guilt or pressure.

What Happens in an Anxiety Counselling Session?

Many first-timers feel nervous, and that is completely normal. Here is what you can usually expect.

  • A warm welcome. You share what has been bothering you, at your own pace.
  • Gentle questions. Your counsellor learns about your feelings, daily routine, and goals.
  • A plan that fits you. Together, you decide what to work on first.
  • Simple tools to practise. You may get small exercises to try between sessions.

There is no pressure to say everything on day one. Progress happens step by step.

7 Simple Steps to Ease Social Media Anxiety Today

While counselling gives you deeper support, these small habits can help you start feeling better right away.

  1. Notice how you feel. After scrolling, pause and ask, "Do I feel better or worse?"
  2. Clean up your feed. Mute or unfollow accounts that leave you feeling low. Follow ones that inspire or teach you.
  3. Set a time limit. Use your phone's screen-time settings to keep scrolling in check.
  4. Create phone-free zones. Try keeping your phone away during meals and the first hour of the morning.
  5. Remind yourself it is a highlight reel. Behind every perfect photo is a real, messy life.
  6. Practise gratitude. Write down three things in your own life that you appreciate each day.
  7. Talk to someone. Share your feelings with a trusted friend or a professional counsellor.

When Should You Consider Counselling?

It may be time to reach out if you notice that:

  • Your worry about social media is affecting your sleep, work, or studies
  • You feel sad, irritable, or empty most days
  • You avoid people or events because you feel you do not measure up
  • You cannot stop comparing yourself, even when you try
  • Self-help tips are not enough anymore

You do not need to wait until things feel unbearable. Reaching out early is a sign of strength, not weakness.

For many people dealing with anxiety, online therapy works very well. What matters most is the connection with your counsellor and your willingness to take part in the process.

How many sessions will I need?

Do I have to quit social media to feel better?

Not at all. The goal is not to remove social media from your life, but to change how you use it and how it makes you feel.

Yes. Your privacy matters. Sessions are private, and your personal information is handled with care.
